# PlanIQ — AI/ML Forecasting

> **Level:** L3 · **Area:** Advanced Features · **PLANS:** Necessary

A formula-based forecast says "next year = this year × (1 + growth %)" — you supply the growth rate
and the model spreads it. That works, but it only knows what *you* tell it. **PlanIQ** is Anaplan's
**AI/ML forecasting** capability: instead of a growth-rate assumption, it **trains a forecast model
on your historical data** and produces statistical predictions — capturing trend, seasonality, and
patterns a human wouldn't reliably eyeball — then writes those predictions **back into your planning
modules** for your normal logic to consume.

> ⚠️ **PlanIQ is a separately-licensed / separately-enabled capability.** It is not part of base
> model building. Reach for it when statistical accuracy is worth the added complexity — not as a
> default. *Confirm current licensing, available algorithms, and limits in Anapedia.*

## What it does, end to end

1. **Feed it data.** PlanIQ uses up to three data inputs. Only the first is required:
   - **Historical data** *(required)* — the actuals time series you want to forecast (e.g. monthly
     sales by product).
   - **Related data** *(optional)* — other time series that influence the outcome (price, promotions,
     weather) to improve accuracy.
   - **Attribute / item metadata** *(optional)* — static descriptors of each item (category, region).
2. **Train a forecast model.** PlanIQ trains on the history and reports accuracy metrics so you can
   compare candidates. You can let **AutoML / Ensemble** pick and blend algorithms automatically, or
   choose a specific one — e.g. **Prophet, DeepAR+, ARIMA, ETS, CNN-QR, MVLR**. *(Algorithm list is
   version-specific — confirm in Anapedia.)*
3. **Generate a forecast.** Run a **forecast action** that produces predictions for the horizon you
   ask for.
4. **Bring it back into the model.** The forecast lands in an Anaplan module (typically a
   [Data or Input module](../03-methodology/disco.md)) where your planning logic — overrides,
   roll-ups, scenarios — takes over. The planner can still adjust the numbers; PlanIQ gives the
   *starting* forecast, not the final plan.

```
History (+ related + attributes)  ──►  PlanIQ trains a model  ──►  forecast action
                                                                        │
                            forecast values written back into  ──►  Anaplan module (D / I)
                                                                        │
                                                  normal planning logic, overrides, reporting
```

## When to use ML forecasting vs a formula

This is the decision a beginner actually has to make. Both are legitimate; pick by fit.

| Use **PlanIQ (ML)** when… | Use a **formula** when… |
| --- | --- |
| You have a **good length of clean history** (the more, the better). | History is short, sparse, or unreliable. |
| The series has **seasonality / trend** worth detecting statistically. | The driver is a known business assumption (a negotiated growth rate, a signed contract). |
| There are **many series** to forecast (thousands of SKUs) — too many to tune by hand. | A handful of lines a planner reasons about directly. |
| Accuracy materially affects the plan (demand planning, revenue). | A simple, transparent rule is "good enough" and easier to defend. |
| Statistical lift justifies the extra licence + complexity. | **Auditability/simplicity matters more** than squeezing out accuracy. |

> A common, healthy pattern: **PlanIQ produces the baseline forecast; planners adjust it** with
> formula-driven overrides for things the model can't know (a new product launch, a one-off event).
> ML and formulas are partners, not rivals.

## Typical use cases

- **Demand forecasting / demand planning** in supply chain — the headline use case.
- **Sales / revenue forecasting** across many accounts or products.
- Any high-volume, history-rich series where a growth-rate assumption is too blunt.

## Beginner watch-outs

- **Garbage in, garbage out.** ML amplifies bad history — clean, de-duplicated, well-aligned actuals
  (ideally sourced from your [Data Hub](data-hub-architecture.md)) matter more than the algorithm.
- **It's a periodic run, not a live recalc.** A forecast is generated by an action on a cadence;
  it doesn't recompute the instant an actual changes.
- **Necessary (PLANS).** Don't bolt on ML where a transparent formula already meets the business
  need — you'd add cost and reduce auditability for no real gain.
- **Explainability.** A formula is self-documenting; an ML forecast is not. Be ready to show
  accuracy metrics and let planners override.
